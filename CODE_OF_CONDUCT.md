# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want this project to be a welcoming place for everyone, whatever their background, experience, age, nationality, religion, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, or appearance. We value patient, respectful and curious conversation about software, and we are happy to help people learn.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that helps build a good community:

- Be kind and considerate. Assume good intent, and ask before you assume otherwise.
- Explain your reasoning. Criticise ideas, not people.
- Accept feedback on your work gracefully, and give feedback that you would want to receive.
- Credit others for their work and their ideas.
- Respect that people have different levels of experience and different first languages.

Examples of behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Harassment, insults, personal attacks or derogatory comments, in any form.
- Sexualised content or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information (such as their address, phone number or private messages) without permission.
- Trolling, deliberate disruption, or repeatedly pushing a point after it has been answered.
- Pressuring people to accept a change, a pull request or a decision.
- Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, commit messages, documentation, and any public or private conversation about the project. It also applies when you represent the project in public.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it to the project maintainer through GitHub: open a private security advisory on the repository (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for the link) and describe what happened, when, and where. If the matter is urgent or involves a risk to someone's safety, say so in the report.

Reports are handled confidentially. We will acknowledge your report, review the facts, and decide what action is needed. You will be told the outcome to the extent that is appropriate. We will not retaliate against anyone who reports in good faith.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides how to respond. Possible responses, depending on severity, include:

1. A private message explaining the problem.
2. A public warning.
3. A temporary or permanent ban from project spaces.

The maintainer may act without warning when the behaviour is severe.
